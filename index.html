Whole Person& Population Modelling

Modelling the whole person, and whole populations, to understand brain health and mental illness across the lifespan.

We integrate genomics, neuroimaging, wearable and clinical data with machine learning to discover who is at risk for mental illness and neurodegeneration, and why, across the lifespan, from youth to late life.

Led by Daniel Felsky, PhD · Krembil Centre for Neuroinformatics, Centre for Addiction and Mental Health (CAMH) · University of Toronto

Research

What we work on

Our program spans psychiatric and neurodegenerative conditions, united by a single idea: that mental and brain health emerge from the interaction of genes, brain, body, behaviour, and environment. These are our active research themes.

01

Youth Mental Health & the Psychosis Spectrum

Using the Toronto Adolescent & Youth (TAY) CAMH Cohort Study, we model how psychosis-spectrum symptoms emerge in young people and search for blood- and MRI-based markers that predict who is at risk.

TAY CohortNeuroimagingBlood biomarkers
02

Integrated Youth Services & Learning Health Systems

We build federated data platforms and predictive models that help integrated youth-mental-health services learn from their own data, improving care, equity, and measurement across Ontario and nationally.

YWHO / IYSEHR & service dataEquity
03

Late-Life Depression, Treatment Response & Dementia Risk

We develop genomic, neuroimaging, and sleep-based machine-learning models to predict antidepressant response and treatment-resistant depression in older adults, and to map the shared roots of depression and dementia.

Machine learningPolygenic scoresMAPLE-D
04

Addictions & Cognitive Dysfunction

Through the Cognitive Dysfunction in the Addictions (CDiA) program, we combine genetics, blood, MRI, and wearable data to understand why some people with substance-use disorders lose cognitive function over time.

CDiAWearablesIntegrative modeling
05

Alzheimer's Disease, Neurodegeneration & Brain Aging Genomics

Our core genomics program builds polygenic, transcriptomic, and single-cell models of risk and resilience in Alzheimer's disease and cognitive aging, spanning the gut-immune-brain axis to tau spreading and brain reserve.

ROS/MAP & ADNISingle-nucleus RNA-seqPET & PRS
06

Bipolar Disorder

We pair single-cell transcriptomics with genetics to build gene-program-specific risk scores for bipolar disorder, and study its neuroimaging correlates and sex-specific cardiovascular comorbidity.

snRNA-seqGWASSex differences
07

Body Image & Eating Disorders

With collaborators, we are building a comprehensive brain-and-behaviour model of how the self-body image forms, while discovering biomarkers of its disturbance in eating disorders and body dysmorphic disorder.

Functional MRIBehaviour
08

AI Methods, Digital Twins & Reproducible Frameworks

We develop reproducible AI benchmarking platforms, digital-twin approaches to neurodegeneration risk and prevention, and open tools for multi-omic data integration (similarity network fusion).

Digital twinsmetaSNFOpen science
09

Environment & Gene-Environment Interaction

How environmental exposures and early-life experience interact with genetic risk to shape brain development and mental health, including prenatal air pollution and childhood adversity. This is the environment pillar of whole-person modelling.

Gene-environmentEarly-life exposuresChild development

Krembil Centre for Neuroinformatics

The Krembil Centre for Neuroinformatics (KCNI) at the Centre for Addiction and Mental Health brings together computational neuroscience, data science, and clinical research to understand the brain basis of mental illness and to accelerate discovery through open, data-driven methods. Our group is part of the KCNI community.
